Public Health and Medical Anthropology Curriculum Development

University of Memphis Assistant Professor of Medical Anthropology August 2019 – May 2021

Project Context

As an Assistant Professor of Medical Anthropology at the University of Memphis from August 2019 to May 2021, Fayana combined teaching, curriculum development, program assessment, and student mentoring.

Need

Students needed courses that connected anthropological research with applied public health concerns, taught through engaging methods and tied to measurable learning objectives. The program also needed faculty contributions to curriculum review, assessment, and administrative planning.

Goals

  • Design and deliver courses on race, health disparities, and applied medical anthropology.
  • Connect course content to measurable learning objectives.
  • Support curriculum review, program assessment, and administrative planning.
  • Mentor students in research and community engagement.

Stakeholders

Students, departmental faculty and administrators, and the communities connected to applied coursework.

Scope

Course design and instruction, curriculum and instructional material development, program assessment support, and student mentoring.

Responsibilities

  • Designed and taught six undergraduate and graduate courses related to race, health disparities, and applied medical anthropology.
  • Developed learning management system materials and interactive learning activities aligned with course objectives using instructional design principles.
  • Developed in-class surveys to assess comprehension and satisfaction, and revised course content based on student feedback.
  • Supported curriculum review and administrative planning through committee service.
  • Supervised six undergraduate and graduate student practicum placements.
  • Published peer-reviewed work on grandparent caregiving and presented at national and international conferences.

Planning

Curriculum development began with learning objectives, then built instructional materials, activities, and assessments aligned to them.

Implementation

Courses used interactive and discussion-based methods suited to demanding topics such as race and health disparities, and mentoring extended learning into research and community engagement.

Research and Data Methods

Curriculum design, learning assessment, interactive and discussion-based instruction, and program assessment.

Deliverables

  • Six undergraduate and graduate courses, designed and taught
  • Learning management system materials and interactive learning activities
  • Contributions to curriculum review and program assessment

Risks and Constraints

Teaching on race and health disparities requires careful facilitation to keep discussions productive and inclusive.

Results

  • Designed and taught six undergraduate and graduate courses.
  • Supervised six undergraduate and graduate student practicum placements, strengthening students' applied research and implementation skills.
  • Published peer-reviewed work on grandparent caregiving and the social and cultural impact of caregiving on aging.
  • Secured funding for a research project on caregiving and social isolation in Memphis, Tennessee, and Detroit, Michigan.

Course titles and teaching evaluation results are not published here pending verification.

Lessons

Designing curricula around measurable objectives reinforced the same discipline Fayana applies to program design: define the goal first, then build the work plan to reach it.

Confidentiality

Student identities and individual student outcomes are never published.
