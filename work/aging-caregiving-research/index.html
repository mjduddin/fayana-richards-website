Community-Engaged Research on Aging, Caregiving, and Chronic Illness

Michigan State University Doctoral Researcher 2010 – 2017

Project Context

As a doctoral researcher in medical anthropology at Michigan State University, Fayana designed and conducted an extended community-based research project in Detroit between 2010 and 2017.

Need

Health disparities affecting older Black women are shaped by social and structural conditions that are difficult to capture without sustained, community-engaged research. The project required careful research design, long-term field engagement, and respectful relationships with participants.

Goals

  • Understand how aging, caregiving, and chronic illness are experienced by older Black women in Detroit.
  • Examine the social and structural determinants shaping those experiences.
  • Produce rigorous scholarly work grounded in community-engaged methods.

Stakeholders

Research participants, community members in Detroit, university faculty and advisors, and funding organizations.

Scope

Approximately 18 months of field research, including qualitative data collection, interviews, and analysis of qualitative and quantitative information.

Responsibilities

  • Designed and conducted field-based research over approximately 18 months.
  • Conducted interviews and collected qualitative information.
  • Collected or analyzed quantitative information.
  • Examined social and structural determinants of health.
  • Produced scholarly work and developed expertise in research design, data collection, analysis, and research communication.

Planning

The research required a defined design, participant engagement approach, and data collection plan developed through doctoral training and supported by competitive funding.

Implementation

Fayana carried out sustained fieldwork in Detroit, building the relationships and trust needed for community-engaged research on sensitive topics including illness, caregiving, and aging.

Research and Data Methods

Ethnographic field research, qualitative interviews, and analysis of qualitative and quantitative information.

Deliverables

  • Completed doctoral research and dissertation (2018 PhD conferral)
  • Scholarly work based on the research

Risks and Constraints

Research involving older adults and sensitive health topics required careful attention to participant privacy, respectful engagement, and accurate representation of findings.

Results

  • The research was supported by multi-year funding from Michigan State University and the National Science Foundation, including an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship (2011–2014).
  • The work led to the completion of her Doctor of Philosophy in Medical Anthropology in 2018.

Lessons

Sustained field research strengthened Fayana's conviction that reliable evidence and lived experience belong together in public health work — a perspective she carries into program design and evaluation.

Confidentiality

Participant identities, the dissertation title, participant counts, award amounts, and detailed findings are not published here pending verification and approval. Research participants are never identified.
